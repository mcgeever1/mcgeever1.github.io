## Indicators

There are maybe four full buidlings visible from the glipse of the skyline you get from my apartment. A mix of shorter residential buildings and their much larger office counterparts, still close enough to make out slight detials in their facades. If you crane your neck you can even see the top of the prudiential, flashing whataver color combination reflects the city's current events.

Red White and Blue for the 4th and Patriots day, White and Green for the Celtics, and many three colored mixes representing the heraldic tinctures of each day's related country.

Below it, much further down is another colored top, much more reserved in its spectacle.
I had heard of a weather indicating building among those in the skyline, and only when I started focusing on its display did I understand that this was the buidling I had heard about.

The information I had been told went something like this:

For decades, a weather beacon utop a skyscaper in Boston has indicated the weather forecast. Blue meant clear skies ahead, flashing for clouds. Red meant rain was to be expected, flashing when that percipation became snow.

A rhyme was developed by bostionians to use as a mnemonic:

> Steady blue, clear view\
> Flashing blue, clouds due\
> Steady red, rain ahead\
> Flashing red, snow instead\

To most in the city, the light would go unnoticed. In the decades following its installation, the information provided by the beacon would be ecplised, first by radio, then the newstream, and eventually smartphones.

For most who live in the city today, they see the beacon among all of the other lights now present and think nothing of it.
